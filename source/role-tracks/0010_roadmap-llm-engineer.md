The LLM Engineer works on the models themselves — fine-tuning, serving, optimisation. It's the deepest specialisation, so build Python + data fluency first, then go deep on models.

## Phase 1 — Strong Python foundation
You'll live in Python and PyTorch.
**Study now:** [Python 1 — the basics](#/page/py-basics) · [Python 2 — pandas & data](#/page/py-data)

## Phase 2 — Understand LLMs deeply
Tokens, context windows, the transformer, attention, temperature.
**Study now:** [GenAI 1 — how LLMs work & prompting](#/page/genai-llm-basics)

## Phase 3 — RAG, embeddings & vectors
Even model specialists must know retrieval — it's how models meet real data.
**Study now:** [GenAI 2 — embeddings, RAG, agents & fine-tuning](#/page/genai-rag)

## Phase 4 — Fine-tuning (LoRA / QLoRA)
Teach models new behaviour efficiently.
**Study now:** [Fine-tuning section in GenAI 2](#/page/genai-rag) · [GenAI vs LLM Engineer — the role](#/page/role-genai-llm-engineer)

## Phase 5 — Serving & optimisation
Run models cheaply and fast in production.
**Study now:** [System design: HLD vs LLD](#/page/hld-lld) · [CAP & distributed systems](#/page/cap-distributed)

<div class="callout callout-warn"><span class="cfor"></span><div>This is the most maths/infra-heavy role. Topics beyond this academy you'll need: deep learning with <b>PyTorch</b>, the Hugging Face stack (<b>transformers + PEFT</b>), <b>GPUs</b>, quantization, <b>vLLM</b>, and distributed training (<b>Ray/DeepSpeed</b>). Most jobs today are actually GenAI-Engineer-level — go there first if unsure.</div></div>
