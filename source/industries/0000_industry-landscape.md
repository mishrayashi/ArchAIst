When people say "I work in tech," they could mean wildly different jobs at wildly different *kinds* of companies. Two engineers with the same title — "Data Engineer" — can have completely different days depending on the **type of company** they work for. Understanding these types early helps you pick a first job, read a job description correctly, and answer the interview question *"why do you want to work here?"* convincingly.

<div class="eli"><span class="eli-tag">Explain like I'm new</span>
A "company type" is just <em>how a company makes money and who it sells to</em>. A restaurant that cooks its own signature dishes (a <strong>product</strong>) is different from a catering company that cooks whatever each client orders (a <strong>service</strong>). Same skill — cooking — very different day.
</div>

## The three big buckets you'll hear about

| Type | How it makes money | Classic examples |
|------|--------------------|------------------|
| **Product-based** | Builds its *own* product/platform and sells it to many users | Google, Netflix, Flipkart, a SaaS startup |
| **Service-based** | Builds/maintains software *for other companies* (clients) | Infosys, TCS, Accenture, Capgemini |
| **Domain-based** (e.g. FinTech, HealthTech) | A product *or* service company focused on one industry | Razorpay (FinTech), a hospital-analytics firm (HealthTech) |

The key insight beginners miss: **these buckets overlap.** "FinTech" isn't the *opposite* of "product-based" — a FinTech like Stripe **is** a product company that happens to work in finance. "Domain" describes *what field* you're in; "product vs service" describes *how the company operates*. A company sits in both at once.

<div class="callout callout-note"><span class="cfor"></span><div><span class="ctitle">Two questions, not one:</span> For any company ask (1) <em>Product or service?</em> (how they operate) and (2) <em>What domain?</em> (finance, retail, health, generic). Razorpay = product + FinTech. Infosys building a bank's app = service + FinTech-adjacent. Netflix = product + media/streaming.</div></div>

## Why this matters for *data & AI* work specifically

Data and AI roles exist in **all** of these, but the work differs:

- **Product company** → you own data pipelines/ML for *one* evolving product. You go **deep**: the same domain, same data, for years. You see your features reach millions of users.
- **Service company** → you build data solutions for *many* clients across industries. You go **broad**: new domain, new stack every project. Excellent for learning fast early in your career.
- **Domain company (FinTech/HealthTech…)** → the *domain rules* shape everything: compliance, accuracy, latency, privacy. A FinTech data engineer thinks about fraud, reconciliation, and audit trails daily.

<div class="pro"><span class="pro-tag">Going deeper · for the experienced</span>
The product/service split also predicts your <strong>engineering maturity exposure</strong>. Product companies tend to invest in platform, internal tooling, and long-lived data contracts (you'll meet feature stores, data mesh, SLAs on datasets). Classic service engagements optimise for delivery against a client SOW, so you may rotate stacks and inherit legacy — great for breadth, but you must be deliberate about depth. Many seniors deliberately zig-zag: service first for breadth, product later for depth and ownership.
</div>

Each domain is a *flavour* layered on top of the product vs service way of working.

> **Key takeaway:** Describe any company with **two** dimensions — *how it operates* (product vs service) and *what domain it serves* (FinTech, retail, health…). They overlap; they're not rivals. The next lessons compare them in depth and help you choose.
